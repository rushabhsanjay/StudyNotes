1. Definition and purpose

Installation is the controlled process of putting a released document, instruction, configuration package, or associated information into use at its intended location, equipment, process, or organizational function.

In document control, installation does not necessarily mean physically installing a machine or component. It means ensuring that the released information is correctly implemented and available where it is intended to be used.

For industrial maintenance and automation, this may involve:

  • Placing a released electrical schematic at the correct machine or in the designated electronic repository.
  • Updating the controlled preventive maintenance procedure used by technicians.
  • Installing a revised machine operating instruction at the operator workstation.
  • Deploying approved PLC or HMI documentation to the appropriate machine knowledge base.
  • Updating the applicable CNC maintenance procedure and machine parameter records.
  • Making a released OT network diagram available to authorized support personnel.
  • Ensuring that obsolete copies are removed from active use.
  • Confirming that affected employees receive required training or acknowledgment.

The main objective is to ensure that the released document is correctly applied at the intended point of use.

A document sitting in a central folder is not necessarily installed. A technician using an outdated printed copy while the new revision exists on a server is a classic example of document control that works beautifully on paper and nowhere else.

2. Position in the document lifecycle

A typical document lifecycle is:

Draft
  ↓
Review
  ↓
Approval
  ↓
Release
  ↓
Installation / Deployment
  ↓
Use and Maintenance
  ↓
Modification
  ↓
Review and Approval of Changes
  ↓
Release and Reinstallation, where required
  ↓
Withdrawal and Archival

The exact lifecycle depends on the organization's document-control procedure.

Stage Main purpose Typical result
Draft Prepare document content Initial version
Review Check correctness and completeness Review disposition
Approval Obtain formal authorization Approved revision
Release Formally issue the approved revision Released document
Installation Apply the released document at the intended location Updated point of use
Use Follow the applicable instruction Work or operational records
Modification Request or initiate a controlled change Change request
Re-release Issue the revised approved document New released revision
Reinstallation Update affected points of use Updated local copies or systems
Withdrawal Stop active use of a document Withdrawn or obsolete status

Release and installation are different.

Release establishes the document's official status and availability. Installation establishes that it has been placed into use or made applicable at the required location.

For example, releasing Revision C of a CNC maintenance procedure does not prove that the maintenance workstation, printed job pack, and machine-specific document index have all been updated to Revision C.

Installation must be traceable to the correct document, revision, and intended location. A generic entry such as "updated documents" is weak evidence because it does not identify what was updated or where.

4. Scope of document installation

Installation can take several forms depending on the type of document and how it is used.

4.1 Physical installation

Examples include:

  • Replacing a controlled printed schematic in an electrical panel documentation pocket.
  • Installing a revised safety instruction at a designated operator station.
  • Replacing an obsolete maintenance checklist in a controlled work folder.
  • Updating a printed inspection standard at a quality inspection station.

Physical installation requires control over old copies, correct placement, legibility, and protection against damage.

4.2 Electronic installation

Examples include:

  • Publishing a released SOP to the authoritative document repository.
  • Updating a machine-specific document index.
  • Making a released troubleshooting guide accessible from an authorized maintenance workstation.
  • Updating a controlled document library used by the maintenance team.
  • Publishing a revised OT network topology document to the designated engineering repository.

Electronic installation should preserve document identity, revision information, access controls, and traceability.

4.3 Software and configuration deployment

For automation systems, the controlled artifact may be a PLC project, HMI application, robot program, CNC parameter set, or network configuration.

In these cases, installation or deployment may include transferring an approved artifact into the intended environment. This is more technically consequential than simply placing a PDF in a folder.

The process may require:

  • Confirmation of target equipment and controller.
  • Verification of the artifact version.
  • Approved change authorization.
  • Backup of the previous state.
  • Defined implementation steps.
  • Required safety and operational precautions.
  • Testing and validation.
  • Rollback arrangements.
  • Installation and closure records.

A document describing a PLC change and the actual PLC program are different controlled items. Updating one does not automatically update the other.

5. Responsibilities during installation

Role Responsibility
Document owner Confirms the document's intended scope and applicable locations

Department supervisor Coordinates implementation at the affected work areas
Maintenance engineer Verifies that relevant maintenance locations and equipment records are updated
Controls engineer Verifies correct use of automation documents and controlled technical artifacts
Safety or quality representative Confirms relevant safety or quality implementation requirements
IT or OT administrator Supports controlled electronic deployment and access where applicable
Technician or operator Uses the applicable revision and reports discrepancies
Training coordinator or responsible supervisor Ensures required training and acknowledgment are completed

The same individual may perform multiple responsibilities if the organization's procedure permits it. However, technical implementation must not bypass required approvals or safety controls.

6. Standard installation workflow

Step 1: Receive the released document

Obtain the document through the approved distribution method.

Verify:

  • Document ID.
  • Title.
  • Revision.
  • Release status.
  • Effective date.
  • Applicable machine, line, department, or process.
  • Required attachments.
  • Any implementation restrictions.

Do not rely only on a filename or email subject. The authoritative document identifier and status must be checked.

Step 2: Identify all affected locations

Determine every point of use that must be updated.

For a maintenance procedure, this may include the central repository, workshop workstation, machine-side controlled copy, PM job pack, and machine knowledge base.

For an electrical drawing, it may include the engineering repository, panel documentation pocket, and maintenance reference system.

The impact assessment should also identify related documents that may need updating.

Step 3: Plan installation

Define:

  • Responsible person.
  • Target locations.
  • Planned installation date.
  • Required access or downtime.
  • Training and communication requirements.
  • Removal of obsolete copies.
  • Verification method.
  • Required installation evidence.

For software or configuration deployment, include backup, implementation authorization, test requirements, and recovery arrangements.

Step 4: Verify the target location

Before placing the document into use, verify that the intended equipment, process, or location matches the document's scope.

For example, an electrical schematic for Machine A must not be installed as the active schematic for Machine B simply because the panels look similar.

Step 5: Install or deploy the released revision

Perform the required action using the approved process.

Examples include publishing a controlled electronic file, replacing a printed document, updating a controlled document index, or deploying an authorized configuration artifact.

Where technical implementation is involved, follow the relevant work permits, isolation procedures, access controls, and implementation instructions.

Step 6: Remove or control superseded copies

Identify the previous revision and remove it from active use.

Depending on the procedure, the old copy may be:

  • Removed and destroyed.
  • Marked as obsolete and archived.
  • Retained as a historical record.
  • Restricted to authorized reference use.
  • Replaced in a controlled electronic system.

Historical retention must not cause obsolete documents to appear as current instructions.

Step 7: Verify installation

Confirm that the correct revision is present at the intended location and can be accessed by authorized users.

Verification may include:

  • Checking the displayed revision number.
  • Comparing the local copy with the authoritative version.
  • Checking document links and attachments.
  • Confirming access permissions.
  • Inspecting the physical location.
  • Verifying that obsolete copies have been removed.
  • Confirming that required labels or indexes have been updated.

Step 8: Complete installation records

Record the required details, including the document ID, revision, location, date, responsible person, and verification result.

Step 9: Complete handover

Notify affected users, complete required training or acknowledgment, and return the process to normal use according to the organization's procedure.

If installation reveals an error, mismatch, or missing requirement, stop the affected implementation where necessary and raise the appropriate discrepancy or change request.

7. Installation record and traceability

An installation record provides evidence that the intended document was made available or applicable at the specified location.

A useful record includes:

Field Purpose
Document ID Uniquely identifies the document
Document title Describes its content
Revision Identifies the exact version installed
Release reference Links the installation to the controlled release
Target location Identifies the machine, workstation, department, or repository
Installation date Records when the action occurred
Responsible person Identifies who performed the installation
Verification result Confirms whether installation met the requirements
Previous revision Identifies the version replaced, if applicable
Obsolete-copy disposition Records removal, archival, or other controlled treatment
Training or acknowledgment status Records completion where required
Remarks or deviations Documents exceptions and corrective actions

Example installation record

Field Example
Document ID CNC-MNT-024
Title CNC Spindle Preventive Maintenance Procedure
Revision Rev C
Target location Machining Line, Machine CNC-04
Installation date 2026-10-18
Responsible person Maintenance Engineer
Previous revision Rev B
Verification Rev C confirmed at designated point of use
Obsolete-copy disposition Rev B removed from active-use folder
Status Installed and verified

This is an illustrative record, not evidence of an actual installation.

8. Point-of-use control

Point-of-use control is the practice of ensuring that the correct controlled information is available where the work is performed.

It is particularly important in maintenance, manufacturing, and quality operations because personnel often work from local copies rather than returning to a central repository for every task.

A point-of-use register can include:

Equipment ID Document type Document ID Current revision Location Verification frequency
H7-TB-01 Test-bench operating procedure TB-SOP-001 Rev D Test-bench workstation As defined by procedure
CNC-04 Electrical schematic CNC-EL-004 Rev B Engineering repository and designated machine reference As defined by procedure
PLC-LINE-02 PLC backup and recovery procedure PLC-REC-002 Rev C Authorized engineering repository As defined by procedure

These identifiers and revisions are illustrative.

The register should be linked to the actual controlled records. A manually maintained table that is never reconciled against the master repository can itself become obsolete.

Practical controls

  • Use a unique equipment or machine ID.
  • Link each document to its authoritative source.
  • Display revision information clearly.
  • Restrict uncontrolled editing.
  • Define who verifies point-of-use currency.
  • Record discrepancies and corrective actions.
  • Include temporary workstations and remote maintenance locations where relevant.

9. Installation of industrial automation documents

9.1 PLC and HMI documentation

When a released PLC or HMI document is installed in the maintenance knowledge system, verify:

  • Correct PLC or HMI identification.
  • Correct machine and line applicability.
  • Project or application revision.
  • Hardware and firmware compatibility where relevant.
  • I/O list and tag references.
  • Network configuration information.
  • Alarm and fault descriptions.
  • Backup and restoration instructions.
  • Links to associated drawings and test records.
  • Access restrictions for sensitive files.

If an actual PLC project is being deployed to a controller, document installation alone is insufficient. The software change must follow the applicable authorization, backup, testing, safety, and commissioning controls.

9.2 CNC and robot systems

For CNC and robot documentation, confirm:

  • Correct machine and controller.
  • Applicable machine-builder documentation.
  • Approved program or parameter version.
  • Coordinate, tooling, and configuration references where applicable.
  • Required backup and restoration records.
  • Relevant verification and validation evidence.
  • Controlled access to executable or configuration artifacts.

Do not confuse installing a document that describes a parameter change with implementing that parameter change on the machine.

9.3 Electrical drawings

When installing a revised schematic:

  1. Confirm the correct drawing number and revision.
  2. Verify that all required drawing sheets and references are included.
  3. Identify every location where the old drawing is used.
  4. Replace or control the old copies.
  5. Update the drawing index or document repository.
  6. Verify that the correct revision is available at the intended point of use.
  7. Retain the installation evidence.

If the drawing describes an actual wiring modification, the physical work must be performed and verified under the relevant engineering and safety procedures.

9.4 Preventive maintenance procedures

A revised PM procedure should be installed at the locations where technicians schedule, prepare, and perform the task.

Verify:

  • Correct equipment applicability.
  • Maintenance frequency.
  • Tools and consumables.
  • Required isolation and safety precautions.
  • Inspection points and acceptance limits.
  • Abnormal-condition response.
  • Required SAP or CMMS records, where applicable.
  • Removal of the superseded procedure.
  • Required technician communication or training.

If the procedure is linked to a maintenance plan or work-order template, updating the document alone may not update that system configuration. The associated record must be checked separately.

9.5 Safety and quality documents

For safety instructions, risk assessments, inspection plans, and quality control procedures, verify that the current released revision reaches the affected workstations and responsible personnel.

Depending on the document, installation may require:

  • Updated controlled copies.
  • Communication of changed hazards or controls.
  • Training or competency confirmation.
  • Updated inspection criteria.
  • Revised forms or checklists.
  • Changes to related work instructions.
  • Evidence that required safeguards or process controls are implemented.

An installed document is not proof that the physical controls it describes are present and effective. That requires the appropriate inspection or verification.

10. Installation versus implementation versus validation

These terms describe different activities.

Activity Main question Example
Release Has the approved document been formally issued? Revision C is published as released
Installation Has the document been placed or made applicable at its intended location? Revision C is available at the CNC workstation
Implementation Have the specified actions or changes been carried out? The approved maintenance procedure is incorporated into the work process
Verification Is the installation or implementation consistent with the specified requirements? The workstation displays the correct revision
Validation Does the resulting system or process meet its intended purpose under the prescribed conditions? The modified machine sequence performs as intended under required validation tests

Not every document installation requires formal validation. The necessary activities depend on whether the item is simply a reference document or part of a technical change that affects equipment, safety, quality, or operations.

For example, installing a revised network diagram may require checking its availability and accuracy. Implementing a network reconfiguration described by that diagram requires separate technical execution and verification.

11. Handling installation discrepancies

Installation may reveal that the released document does not match the actual equipment or that the intended point of use cannot be updated.

Examples include:

  • The drawing identifies a different PLC model from the installed hardware.
  • The machine-side document pocket contains a revision that does not match the repository.
  • A released procedure refers to a removed component.
  • The intended workstation cannot access the document.
  • A required attachment is missing.
  • The released document references an obsolete software version.
  • A document is assigned to the wrong machine.
  • A safety instruction cannot be applied under the existing site configuration.

The response should be controlled.

  1. Identify the discrepancy and affected location.
  2. Assess immediate operational, quality, and safety implications.
  3. Prevent use of incorrect information where necessary.
  4. Notify the document owner and responsible technical authority.
  5. Raise a discrepancy, nonconformance, or change request as appropriate.
  6. Determine whether the document requires correction, revision, withdrawal, or reapproval.
  7. Record the corrective action and verify closure.

Do not silently edit a released document to make it match the machine. That creates a second version with no reliable approval history.

12. Installation of temporary documents

Temporary instructions may be needed during a breakdown, trial, engineering change, or commissioning activity.

A temporary document should have clearly defined controls:

  • Unique identification or reference.
  • Authorization under the applicable procedure.
  • Scope and applicable equipment.
  • Explicit limitations.
  • Issue and expiry or review dates.
  • Responsible owner.
  • Required safety precautions.
  • Method of communication.
  • Removal or replacement arrangements.
  • Record of the final disposition.

Temporary instructions must not remain in circulation indefinitely simply because nobody remembers who created them.

Where a temporary instruction affects a safety-critical function, machine configuration, or quality acceptance criterion, the applicable risk assessment and authorization requirements remain in force.

14. Installation performance indicators

Installation effectiveness can be measured using defined performance indicators.

KPI Calculation Purpose
Installation completion rate Completed required installations divided by total required installations, multiplied by 100 Measures completion against the plan
First-pass installation success Installations passing verification without rework divided by total installations, multiplied by 100 Measures installation quality
Average installation lead time Total elapsed installation time divided by completed installations Measures implementation speed
Obsolete-copy incidents Confirmed incidents of obsolete-document use during the period Measures point-of-use control effectiveness
Verification completion rate Installations with completed verification divided by installations requiring verification, multiplied by 100 Measures evidence completeness
Overdue installations Count of required installations not completed by their due date Highlights outstanding risk
Acknowledgment completion rate Required acknowledgments completed divided by total required acknowledgments, multiplied by 100 Measures communication completion where applicable

For example:

\[ \text{Installation completion rate} = \frac{\text{Completed installations}}{\text{Required installations}} \times 100\\% \]

If 48 out of 50 required installations are completed, the installation completion rate is \(96\\%\).

A high completion rate is not enough by itself. If the wrong revision is installed at even one safety-critical location, the remaining risk may still be unacceptable.

15. Practical example: updating an engine test-bench maintenance procedure

A revised maintenance procedure is released to correct the troubleshooting instructions for a recurring sensor communication fault.

The installation process is:

  1. Identify every test bench covered by the procedure.
  2. Check the released document ID, revision, and effective date.
  3. Confirm the intended installation locations.
  4. Publish the document to the authoritative repository.
  5. Update the controlled machine-specific document index.
  6. Replace printed copies where controlled hard copies are required.
  7. Remove or mark the previous revision as superseded.
  8. Notify the relevant maintenance personnel.
  9. Complete required training or acknowledgment.
  10. Verify that the correct revision is accessible at each designated point of use.
  11. Record installation date, location, responsible person, and verification result.
  12. Close outstanding installation actions or raise discrepancies.

If the revised procedure also requires a change to PLC logic, that logic change must be handled through its own controlled technical modification and implementation process. The installation of the procedure does not authorize the PLC change.

16. Practical checklist

Before installation

  • Correct released document received.
  • Document ID, revision, and effective date verified.
  • Target machines, departments, and workstations identified.
  • Supporting documents and attachments available.
  • Implementation requirements and responsibilities defined.
  • Required safety, access, or downtime arrangements established.
  • Training and acknowledgment requirements identified.

During installation

  • Correct revision placed at each intended location.
  • Document applicability checked against equipment or process.
  • Electronic access and links verified.
  • Superseded copies removed or controlled.
  • Related indexes and records updated.
  • Technical deployment performed only under its required authorization.

After installation

  • Installation record completed.
  • Required verification performed and recorded.
  • Required personnel informed or trained.
  • Discrepancies raised and controlled.
  • Historical versions retained where required.
  • Outstanding actions assigned and tracked.
  • Installation formally closed according to procedure.

17. Interview questions and workplace understanding

Q1. What is document installation?

It is the controlled placement or implementation of a released document at its intended point of use, with verification and records as required.

Q2. What is the difference between release and installation?

Release formally issues the approved revision. Installation ensures that the document is placed or made applicable at the intended location.

Q3. Does installing a document prove that the physical change has been completed?

No. Document installation and physical implementation are separate activities. The actual change must be verified according to the applicable engineering and safety procedures.

Q4. Why is point-of-use control important?

It helps ensure that personnel work from the correct instructions at the machine, workstation, or process where the task is performed.

Q5. What should happen if the released drawing does not match the actual machine?

Report and assess the discrepancy, control the risk, notify the responsible technical authority, and use the formal correction or change-control process. Do not silently modify the released drawing.

Q6. Is an installation record mandatory for every document?

The organization's procedure determines the required record. Higher-risk documents, safety-related instructions, and controlled technical deployments generally require stronger traceability than routine reference material.

Q7. Can a technician install a new revision without approval?

A technician may perform an assigned installation after the required release conditions have been satisfied. The technician must not bypass mandatory approval or independently authorize an unapproved revision.

Q8. What is the difference between a controlled copy and a local copy?

A local copy describes where the document is stored. A controlled copy describes how its identity and status are managed. A local copy can be controlled or uncontrolled.

Q9. How should an obsolete printed drawing be handled?

Remove it from active use, mark and archive it where required, or dispose of it under the established procedure. It should not remain available as if it were current.

Q10. Why is installation important in industrial maintenance?

Because even a technically correct, approved document provides little protection if the intended technician cannot access it, receives the wrong revision, or continues using obsolete instructions.

18. Core principles to remember

  • Installation connects the released document to its intended point of use.
  • The correct document, revision, scope, and effective date must be verified.
  • Superseded copies must be controlled.
  • Installation evidence should identify the document, location, date, and verification result as required.
  • Technical artifacts such as PLC programs and CNC parameters need separate deployment and configuration controls.
  • Training, acknowledgment, and physical implementation are additional requirements when applicable.
  • A discrepancy between the released document and actual equipment must be managed through formal corrective action or change control.
  • Installation is not complete merely because a file has been uploaded.
  • Installation records support troubleshooting, audits, change history, and future maintenance.
